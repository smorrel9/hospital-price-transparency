## Secondary finding: reverse mislabeling (HCPCS-format codes labeled CPT)

Not the pattern the pre-flight was checking for, but surfaced directly by the overlap check's non-overlapping-code list: **270 rows (166 distinct codes)** are labeled `code_type: "CPT"` despite being in classic HCPCS Level II format (one letter + 4 digits) straight from the source CSV. Examples pulled from the parsed file:

```
{"description":"HC DISPOSABLE SUPPLIES ALS - FIXED WING","code":"A0430","code_type":"CPT", ...}
{"description":"HC OXYGEN & OXYGEN SUPPLIES - FIXED WING","code":"A0430","code_type":"CPT", ...}
```

Other affected ranges: `A0425`-`A0436` (ambulance supply codes), `C8900`-`C9789` (device/pass-through codes), `G0279`/`G0539`-`G0543` (screening/preventive codes), `J2765`-`J7168` (drug J-codes), `L1834`-`L3971` (DME/orthotics), `P9020`/`P9073` (blood products), `Q4187`-`Q4196` (skin substitute Q-codes), `U0001`/`U0002` (COVID test codes). This is the opposite direction from the known BSW/Houston-Methodist/Memorial-Hermann promotion pattern (which correctly demotes numeric-looking HCPCS to CPT) and isn't covered by `DEFAULT_PROMOTIONS`. It affects only 270/131,481 rows (0.2%) and 166/7,448 distinct "CPT" codes (2.2%) -- small enough not to block seeding, but worth a follow-up `promotions` rule (`{"from": "CPT", "to": "HCPCS", "pattern": "^[A-Z]\\d{4}$"}`) in a column-map override, especially since Memorial Hermann publishes 18 other facility files that may share this quirk.

## Recommendation

**Proceed to seed.** All five of the primary checks pass: row survival is 100%, the code_type distribution is healthy with no domination or zero-CPT gap, price sanity is clean (no negatives, zero-values explained, spot-checked CPT prices are in-range), null rate (26.6%) is normal, and cross-hospital CPT overlap is strong (99.4% against the hospitals that could be scanned). The two flagged items are minor and non-blocking:

1. A small reverse-direction mislabeling (166 HCPCS-format codes tagged CPT, 0.2% of rows) -- worth a follow-up promotion rule, not a re-parse blocker.
2. Four casing-duplicate payer names (`Unitedhealthcare`/`UnitedHealthCare`, `Bcbs`/`BCBS`, etc.) that will fragment the payer picker -- a Phase 2 payer-name-normalization concern per `database-architecture.md`, not something to fix at parse time.

Also flagging for the human running the pipeline: the cross-hospital overlap baseline in this run excludes `ascension-seton-austin` and `dell-seton-austin` (their parsed files are too large to fully scan at this environment's current ~23MB/s disk throughput, and a prefix sample was non-representative because those files are code-sorted) -- 99.4% overlap against the remaining four hospitals is still a strong positive signal, but a fuller check against those two (e.g. once disk I/O conditions improve, or via a stride-sampled/randomized read rather than a byte-prefix) would make the baseline more complete. Per this task's scope, `data/prices.db` was not read or modified.
